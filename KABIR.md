### MediTrack (live)

Founder & developer · 2025 – now

Personal healthcare assistant for a billion people.

A platform that explains lab reports via a phone call, WhatsApp, or the web. Live in 5 Indian languages and Hinglish (Hindi+English code-switched dialogue).

- 1,500+ lab reports explained
- 3 hospitals using it
- 6 languages, incl. Hinglish

**Built:** It reads any Indian lab report, flags what is off, and informs the user when to see a doctor without a diagnosis.

**Hard part:** How to build a product that makes it accessible and intuitive for the elderly and middle-class in India.

**Learned:** AI can never be democratised if it is not inclusive of language and the communities that dialogue exists in. Technology literacy is very low in India, which is why I embarked on a journey of making 3 prototypes, the last being the most convenient for a typical Indian user.

### This vs That (live)

Creator & developer · 2025 – now

An AI judge that's allowed to say "it depends".

**Built:** A 5-stage pipeline on top of Stanford's Co-STORM model (https://github.com/stanford-oval/storm). It contains fixed rules to pick a verdict, and is not a standalone model.

**Hard part:** My test cases and actual user inputs had a difference in accuracy rates. To fix this, I found a filter that was faulty which threw away a quarter of the conflicts, so I deleted it.

**Learned:** In general, AI is great at facts but horrible with judgement. On complex real-world questions, it's unable to match any perspective that a sentient human brings.

Links: https://thisvsthat.live/ · Code: https://github.com/radcool22/this-vs-that

## research

### Perceptions of AI Voice Explanations for Medical Reports

Author · Oxford Journal of Student Scholarship · Oct 2026

Conducted a field study for MediTrack on whether people would like to hear their medical records being explained in their own language.

**Method:** Surveyed 103 adults and conducted 9 interviews in a rural hospital in Muzaffarnagar.

**Findings:** 99 of the 103 preferred voice, and 81.6% of them struggled with the jargon and language in their reports, often relying on a doctor to clarify their doubts.

**Learned:** Voice is the best method for communicating with unfamiliar technology. Using text becomes unintuitive when voice options exist.

Paper: https://www.oxfordjss.org/oct-2026-vol-12-issue-1/perceptions-of-ai-assisted-voice-explanations-for-medical-diagnostic-reports-in-multilingual-india
